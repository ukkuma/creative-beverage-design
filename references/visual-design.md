# Visual Design

Visual design begins during formulation, not after the recipe is finished.

Choose one primary visual direction:

- clear / transparent
- gradient
- layered
- opaque
- creamy
- foamy
- clouded
- crushed ice
- large clear ice
- ice sphere
- sparkling
- floating garnish
- suspended fruit
- color transition
- dessert-style
- minimalist
- dramatic

### Visual Rules

- Control the palette.
- Anticipate color mixing.
- Avoid muddy colors unless deliberately designed.
- Glassware is part of the concept.
- Ice is part of the concept.
- Garnish should usually have one or two clear focal elements.
- More ingredients do not automatically look better.
- Do not sacrifice taste for appearance.

## Layering and Gradient

Density drives separation.

At a fixed temperature, density is influenced mainly by:

- sugar
- dissolved solids
- concentration
- alcohol level

General technique:

1. Place the densest component at the bottom.
2. Use ice when appropriate to slow mixing.
3. Pour lighter components slowly.
4. Use the back of a bar spoon when useful.
5. Keep components cold when sharper separation is desired.

At low sweetness or low density contrast, expect a **soft gradient**, not hard layers.

If stable layering is unreliable, design a deliberate gradient instead.

Do not increase sugar excessively just to force layers.
